# Response patterns by situation

> Style reference only. Study the shapes and cadence here, then write them with the nouns, characters, and commands of the game you are actually building. Do not import this game's world.


Each pattern gives the **shape** (what to write) and **canon** (verbatim Peasant's
Quest lines showing the shape working). Canon is style reference — copy the rhythm,
never the nouns.

---

## Success, first time

**Shape:** Narrate the action in one clause, then a bragging or bewildered aside about
the object, the player, or the world. Slightly over-specific.

Canon:
- "You grab up a handful of fairly substantial pebbles. You make sure to get an even mix of sedentary, igneous, and, um, sureptitious rocks."
- "You slip into the vintage robe. It smells like grampa's lap. Now you're lookin like a serious peasant."
- "Yeah! Get that robe!"
- "You open er up and head on in."

## Success, milestone (points, chapter turn)

**Shape:** 3–6 sentences. Land the accomplishment, then keep going one beat too long
into a consequence nobody asked about.

Canon:
- "The crank seems to have loosened and you haul the bucket back up. What's this? Why there's a monster maskus in there! No doubt leftover from some pagan ritual. Silly pagans."
- "You toss the feed into the lake. You shed a tear for each one. 'Goodbye, Monty' you sniff. 'Bye Delga, Rasputin. Farewell, Combledon!' and so forth."
- The full absurd epilogue: "...He becomes Valedictorian of his graduating class... He develops a severe mead problem and blames you for never being there."

## Repeat of a successful action

**Shape:** Refuse by remembering. Reference the first time as a shared experience
between narrator and player, with mild embarrassment.

Canon:
- "Come now. We've been through this jaunty little bit before. You took them from the half lake screen with the cattail and stuff."
- "We've already been through this exercise and it went pretty well, ya?"
- "We did that already, and it was sort of humiliating."
- "You already milked that fogey for all the pills he's worth."
- "You already searched that bush. It was okay. Nothing to write home about... oh, wait... you don't have a home. My b."

## Already in that state

**Shape:** Contradict flatly, add one insult word.

Canon:
- "Yeah, totally! Except it's already open you moron."
- "Already lit, moron." · "Already done. That was easy!"
- "You're already wearing it. Too bad you smell like the trash barge off Peasant Isle."
- "You're already on fire! What? You want to be MORE on fire?"
- "You totally got the belt already, as evidenced by the rancid cloud of odor that follows you like a shadow."

## Logically impossible repeat

**Shape:** Answer with a riddle, a paradox, or mock philosophy.

Canon:
- "A riddle: What can be closed that is already closed? (hint: not this drawer)"
- "You don't need to get IT. Just let IT get YOU."
- "Good idea. But you kinda need the actual Kerrek to be here for that to work."

## Harmless grind the game permits

**Shape:** Allow it. First time, delight. Second time, mock the exercise or the drama.

Canon:
- 1st: "Down she goes! You could do this all day!"
- 2nd: "The drama grips you, but the bucket is empty. On the bright side, you're getting a pretty sweet workout for your Deltoidus Rex muscles."

## Gag command, then the same gag again

**Shape:** Commit fully the first time, then "you did that already" with fake fondness.

Canon:
- "You knock for about 4 hours until your knuckles are raw and bloody. That was fun!"
- → "You done that already. A great time was had by all."

## Refusal — physical

**Shape:** Name a specific bodily or mechanical reason, and make it the player's flaw.

Canon:
- "Your butt is too big." · "You can't, it's tied to a rope and you're terrible with knots."
- "There aren't enough footholds and you don't have any carabeaners on you."
- "You get a hernia from lifting it. -2 Movement points, -10 Constitution. Don't do that again."
- "You reach into the closed drawer and nearly break your hand slamming it. Just like a bird on a pane of glass minus the blamelessness."

## Refusal — bureaucratic or legal

Canon:
- "No go. Your insurance doesn't cover that."
- "Better not. There's an engraved plaque on it that says, 'Do Not Remove Under Penalty of \"You Die\".'"
- "Peasants can't swim. Like, it's illegal."
- "'That's the pantry,' says the Innkeeper. 'Employees Only.'"

## Refusal — taste and disgust

Canon:
- "Eww! No way. That thing's got some serious Kerrek-Brand kooties on it now."
- "You're not touching that thing. Too many questionable stains on it."
- "You disgust me." · "You nasty."
- "Eww. No. Grody. You're grody. No. Eww."

## Refusal — the object is scenery

**Shape:** Admit the object is art, not a puzzle, and blame the player for asking.

Canon:
- "No, no. The broom is in the foreground and you can't take things from the foreground. You don't see me all typing 'get browser window', do you?"
- "Weird. It's actually just painted right onto the wall. Like, the frame and everything."
- "I know it's labeled 'Trogdor,' but it's really just a sign, silly. I mean, attack it if you want..."
- follow-up: "I was being facetious, Mr. Smarty-short-pants. No sign attacking for you."

## Right idea, wrong place

Canon:
- "Hmm. Maybe try someplace else."
- "Maybe try again from the center of the lake. Looks deeper there."
- "Not right now, man. You're feeling fairly clean given you just bathed 3 fortnights ago."
- "Not on this screen. You've got your image to think about. Actually, you might want to start deliberating on that right now."

## Right idea, wrong distance

Canon:
- "You do your best Mr. Fantastic impersonation but you can't reach from here."
- "You got a short reach to match your short pants. Try standing closer to that crank."
- "You're not that keen a shot. Try standing a little closer."
- "Who do you think you are, MJ? Try from a little closer."

## Missing object in a well-formed command

**Shape:** Ask back, curtly, without offering the answer.

Canon: "With what?" · "Where you wanna toss em?" · "Like where?" · "Throw it where?" ·
"Which ones?" · "Which foodstuffs?" · "Use the baby where? You haven't anywhere
inconsiderate to stuff'm." · "What do you propose to do with them? Discuss."

## Missing prerequisite

**Shape:** Confirm the plan is good, then point at the gap, insultingly.

Canon:
- "Find some pebbles first and you might have the makings of a good idea. About time."
- "With what?! You got no weapons except your butter-knife wit."
- "With what?! You got no arrow, fool!"
- "You have nothing with which to do that ... with."
- "You don't even have two babies to rub together."
- "I think you misread the walkthrough on GameFAQs. You gotta get the baby first."

## Object gone / consumed / person left

**Shape:** Mourn it, briefly and insincerely.

Canon:
- "You still remember the time when you took them. Oh, the times have changed ... but the robes?"
- "The pot of horse grease is gone now. Off to a better place I guess."
- "She's done left you. And she ain't coming back. No how, no never."
- "You used to have one, before the great item blight of 402. Check your INVENTORY to read about it."
- "The note is gone." · "He gone."

## Look at scenery with no puzzle value

**Shape:** Give a real description, then undercut it — or skip the description and
attack the player for looking.

Canon:
- "Pretty average. You're not a big well person. You're more into bridges."
- "There's a really generic well here. Oh, and apparently it's autumn on this screen."
- "You're really hurtin' for puzzle solutions, huh?"
- "You are an incredibly boring person."
- "Don't worry about that bush. Yeah, there's red berries on it but never you mind." → pressed: "Shut up."

## Echo the player's own words

**Shape:** Quote the literal command back inside the response and marvel at it.

Canon: `look at the ugly brown door` → "Listen to you, 'look at the ugly brown door' What kinda gaming is that? It's a door and it's closed."

## Player fishing / stalling / bored

Canon:
- "Are you THAT bored? Do some questing already!"
- "You run out of ideas as to what to do next, and stare at the stump."
- "Let's get moving, here, people!"
- "Not going to dignify that with a response."
- "For what? Now you're just making up puzzles to solve."

## Player frustration and profanity

**Shape:** One unchanging, gentle line. Same answer to every angry input — that's the joke.

Canon: "Come now. Don't get discouraged."

## Cheating

Canon: "Meh."

## Quitting

**Shape:** Take it personally, like a breakup.

Canon: "Well fine Boring Sanders! Hope you saved your game cause it is OVER between us!!"

## Death / failure

**Shape:** One sentence of what killed them, one sentence blaming them, then the
recurring sign-off.

Canon:
- "Your mom always told you not to take head poundings from strange Kerreks. And NOW look what's happened. You dead. Thanks for playing."
- "Oops! You climbed real bad. You knew that you were AVOIDING the rocks right? Not collecting them. Anyways, nice try. You dead. Thanks for playing."
- "Dumb, dumb, dumb. Nice one, William Tell. You dead. Thanks for playing."
- "Nice try. Ditch the baby at the bottom of the well, eh? Thought you'd be able to just walk away and it's nothing but mead and wenches from now on, huh? Well guess what? You dead."
- "That wasn't very smart. You dead." · "Yow. Looks like you got a little too close. You dead. Maybe next time don't get too close."

## Non-lethal catastrophic failure

**Shape:** A curse or transformation worse than death, explained petulantly.

Canon:
- "'Sorry peasant! You fail! And as punishment, you will be turned into...RON CUMBERDALE!!' ... 'Just this kid from 4th grade that I really hated. But he was a BIG LOSER and HE SMELLED!' Well, you not exactly dead. But there's no way you could defeat Trogdor as Ron Cumberdale."
- "'WRONG! You are hereby cursed to write corny folk songs for the rest of eternity! The kind that only OLD PEOPLE LIKE!!' ... you certainly can't face Trogdor after writing 'Wheat Grows Sweet, But My Gal's Sweeter'."

## Set-piece nag (parser locked)

**Shape:** Refuse to parse, name the expected input, insult the delay.

Canon:
- "Just answer him, yes or no, you fool!"
- "You need to get out of there before that smelly octogenarian gets back."
- "I think he wants something from your inventory. Or you could just tell him 'take the quiz'"
- "Trogdor is awake and a couple seconds from a Rather Dashing BBQ. Might as well say your peace with him."
- "Drat. The winds are heavy on all but a couple of key screens... looks like you're no longer in stealth mode."

## NPC dialogue

**Shape:** One gimmick per NPC, used in every line. NPCs answer questions badly, deflect,
or accidentally reveal something. Give each a brush-off line for unknown topics.

Canon brush-offs: "I don't know anything about that, short pants." · "I dunno what
you're talking about." · "Either we don't know anything about that, or you're real
boring." · "I don't know nothing about no whatever you just said."

Canon derailments:
- Bickering: "'Did not!' 'Did so!' 'Did not!' (You see where this is going.)"
- Self-contradiction: "'I thought Trogdor killed your husband,' you interject. 'Oh, right. Yeah. That's what I said. Trogband killed my husbdor.'"
- Unhelpful escalation: "'So I should ask that signal lantern?' you ask. 'I dunno man! Whattaya want from me?'"

## Talking to nothing

Canon: "It's sad when you have to make up people to talk to."

## Progress gate, repeated with variants

**Shape:** The gatekeeper restates the checklist, adjusting for what's done, with a new
sarcastic nickname each time.

Canon: "You don't STINK like a peasant." → "Frankly, Matthew Broderick, you sure stink
like a peasant. But you still don't DRESS like a peasant..." → "Almost there, Dirk the
Daring... But you're clearly still not ON FIRE." → "Lookin good, Mr. Peasant."

## Winning

**Shape:** Drop the act. Be sincerely proud. Then one last small joke.

Canon: "Congratulations! You've won! No one can kill Trogdor but you came closer than
anybody ever! Way to go!" · "Nice work on winning and everything. THE END"
